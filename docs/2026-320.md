## 10:04 MST

### Checking the model against the data

The Moon looked stuck in the same place whatever the site or the date, so I
checked the math against Horizons. The orbit math was right: the Moon from
Kennedy Space Center matched within a degree on two dates months apart. Most of
what looked stuck was the Earth seen from a Moon site, and that one really
doesn't move much, since the Moon always shows the Earth the same face.

But checking the scene itself, not just the math, found a real bug. From a site,
bodies without sky data were placed out from the body's center instead of the
site. Distances are squashed by a cube root, so starting from the wrong point
bent the direction you see. The Moon from an Earth site was up to 9° off. Now
every body is placed from the site.

To keep it that way, I recorded Horizons answers for every catalog site, on two
dates, and wrote a test that replays them offline. Each body, placed the way the
scene places it, has to sit within 1.5° of Horizons. The worst is under 0.8°,
the Moon from Earth, because I measure from Earth's center and Horizons from the
surface. Putting the old bug back fails 13 tests.

### Terrain is still the gap

The pitch says ephemeris and topography. The ephemeris half is real now, and
tested. Topography is only pictures: the LOLA relief is shown, but no height is
used, so the horizon is a smooth sphere. At the south pole, where the Sun sits a
degree or two up, a ridge can be the whole answer. A horizon profile per site,
from LOLA or MOLA, is the next big step.

### Forms that fit

Latitude and longitude are spin buttons now, like the dates: degrees,
thousandths, and N or S. Arrow keys, the wheel, or typing, and it can only hold
a real coordinate, so the form lost its parsing. The hint under the fields
became a small ? with a tooltip, "You can click in the 3D model", and the window
form got the same treatment for its 31-day limit. Both forms share their fields
and spacing now. Earth visibility became Earthview, one word next to Sunlight,
under Look for (conditions).

### One control size

The bars were 36, 50, or 54 pixels depending on what was in them. Two causes: a
scrollbar under the candidates, and icon buttons using `aspect-square`, which in
a stretched row feeds its height into its width and back. Every button and bar
is at least 44 now, the touch target, and icon buttons are simply that wide. I
also learned the theme wipes Tailwind's own scale, so `px-px` and `font-normal`
were silently doing nothing. There's a `nano` step now, 2px, for the tight
spots.

### A clearer compare

The diff signs read wrong: plus looked like better, and the values have their
own signs, so a line could read `+ +1.0°`. The Compare card is columns now, the
reading I'm on, then the hovered candidate with its change in parentheses, green
for more and red for less. Elevation is an arrow, up or down, with "Sun up" or
"Earth down" in its tooltip. And the first row is each side's date and hour,
which answered my own question of what exactly was being compared.
